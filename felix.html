Felix's page: the shape of the system

Look at the letters first. Then find the rule.

The story

Start with a clock. Ten past eight and quarter to nine both sit on the same face, but the marks on the face are not all the same size: the minutes come in fives, and the hours come in twelves. The steps are mixed, and we read them without thinking.

The Roman letters have the same kind of mixed steps. Look at the seven letters in order: I, V, X, L, C, D, M. Each step is either times five or times two, and they keep alternating:

1 → 5 (×5) → 10 (×2) → 50 (×5) → 100 (×2) → 500 (×5) → 1000 (×2)

Every two steps make times ten. That is the same as the place value we use every day: 47 means 4 tens and 7 ones, and each column is ten times the one before it.

What to notice

  • Each full turn of the spiral is one times ten.
  • V sits about seven-tenths of a turn from I, a little more than halfway round. X comes back to the start.
  • The same shape repeats for every power of ten: I, X, C, M, and V, L, D between them.

Where this leads

  • Decimal. Every column is times ten.
  • Binary. Every column is times two: 1, 2, 4, 8. Computers use only two digits.
  • Algorithms. A rule that you repeat, step by step, to get the answer. Writing a numeral from its parts is one.

The order of the tabs

  1. Spiral. The letters on a spiral, one full turn for each times ten.
  2. Steps. The same letters as a staircase, with the times-five and times-two steps marked.
  3. Tiles. Each letter as a square, drawn to scale by its area.
  4. Rods. A counting board, one rod for each column. Type a number and see its parts, then the Roman numeral they make.

This page explores the shape of the system. It is not part of the lesson unless Jimmy says so. Roman numerals are not place value: each letter has its own value, and there is no zero. The rods show how place value and the letters are related, not how the Romans wrote numbers.

The letters on a spiral

Each full turn is times ten. The letters step round it: the five comes a little more than halfway round, and the ten finishes the turn.

The staircase: times five, times two

Each step up is the next letter. The label between two letters says what the step multiplies by.

The letters to scale

Each square has the area of its letter's value. Five times as much area is a square about 2.2 times as wide. Compare I with M: 1000 times the area.

The counting board

Each rod is one column. The bead on top is five; the beads underneath are one each. Type a number from 1 to 3999, or press Random. The parts come first, then the numeral they make.
